About The Alkane

The Alkane is a free tool that turns a molecule you draw into its IUPAC name. Built for students, it's simple, accessible, and free forever.

What is The Alkane?

Draw an organic structure in the browser and The Alkane names it — or tells you plainly if it can't, rather than guessing. No sign-up, no cost, no limit on how many molecules you name.

What can it name?

Alkanes, alkenes and alkynes, straight or branched, plus rings, benzene, and the functional groups you'll meet at GCSE and A-Level — alcohols, acids, amines, esters and more — with chains up to ten carbons long. Stereochemistry and more complex ring systems aren't covered yet.

The Story and History

I started off tutoring GCSE chemistry at my school, and saw firsthand the struggles students faced grasping the concepts needed for nomenclature. When it clicked, it clicked but students needed a visual aid to practice. That is why I decided to create this tool.

The naming algorithm was written by myself for A-Level Computer Science coursework. The program was written entirely manually and from scratch with each path validated against actual IUPAC guidelines taught in Chemistry GCSE and A-Level. I have since expanded this into the tool here today.

The algorithm covers every molecule you may encounter before university, plus vastly more, so you can explore, discover, and test yourself.

Is it free and open source?

Yes to both. No account, no payment, no install, and free forever. The naming engine is open source on GitHub.
